### Key Concepts

The world 'variety' can be used a catch all phrase to talk about language variation.
### Language or Dialect

There is a difference between a general understanding and an academic understanding of the terms 'language' and 'dialect'.

> The word ‘language’ is used to refer either to a single linguistic norm or to a group of
related norms, and ‘dialect’ is used to refer to one of the norms.

A dialect is often viewed as a lesser version of the standard or highest prestige version of the language.

For most people, the term 'language' is synonymous with the 'standard' form of the language. So it stands for a super-ordinate term and also an example of a sub-ordinate.

Sociolinguists however view all dialects as being equal, including the standard variety.

#### Mutual intelligibility

> The commonly cited criterion used to determine if two varieties are dialects of the
same language or distinct languages is that of mutual intelligibility: if speakers can
understand each other, they are speaking dialects of the same language; if they
cannot, they are speaking different languages.

There are a number of problems with this definition however. Firstly intelligibility is a subjective measure that may differ considerably between individuals. Secondly dialects exist on what is known as a dialect continuum. This means that two speakers of dialects of what is considered to be the same language may not be able to understand each other. Whereas, two dialects of differing languages may be mutually comprehensible. Dutch -> German is a well-known dialect continuum.

There are also numerous examples of distinct languages that are mutually comprehensible, e.g. Urdu -> Hindi, Serbian -> Croatian, Swedish -> Norwegian.

Finally, there are examples of dialects that are non-mutually comprehensible to each other. For example, Cantonese and Mandarin or various varieties of Arabic. 
#### The role of social identity

> Sociolinguists claim that the deining factor in determining whether two varieties
are considered distinct languages or dialects of the same language is sociopolitical
identity, not linguistic similarity or diference. 

### Standardisation

The standard language ideology is that:

> People tend to think of a language as a legitimate and fixed system which can be
objectively described and regard dialects as deviations from this norm.

Languages can become standardised in a variety of ways such as the writing of dictionaries, grammars, the recognition of a body of literature etc.
#### The standard as an abstraction

According to Lippi-Green, belief in the standard language ideology comes with a number of corollary beliefs, e.g that the standard language is a precious inheritance from past generous that needs to be protected from becoming debased. There is a circular argument in favour of standardisation that educated people speak the standardised form and we know that they are educated from the way in which they speak.
#### The standardisation process

Proponents of standardisation often rail against language change and propose ways to fix a language.
#### The standard and language change

This is in opposition to how sociolinguists view language change.
#### Standard English?

Trudgill defines standard English as the variety which is:

- usually used in print
- normally taught in school
- learned by non-native speakers
- spoken by educated people
- used in news broadcasts

Standard English is well-codified at the moment although this is being challenged by the emergence of new varieties of English in Asia.
#### The standard - dialect hierarchy

The interplay between language and dialect often involves the re-imagining of history, e.g. making it seem inevitable that the standard form would become the predominant variety of the language.

There is discussion as to whether the terms language and dialect are synonymous when there is only a single 'dialect' of a uniformly spoken language.

### Regional Dialects
#### Dialect continua

Gerographical differences in language are one of the most noticeable forms of language variation.

> The hardening of political boundaries in the modern world as a
result of the growth of states, particularly nation-states rather than multinational or
multiethnic states, has led to the hardening of language boundaries. 
#### Dialect geography

There is a long tradition of delineating boundaries between different accents. This is often done through the use of isoglosses - lines on a map showing differences in language use between different regions.
#### Everybody has an accent

Just as dialect is not synonymous with formality (standard English can be used informally), so accent different from the idea of dialect.

RP is one of the most dominant accents in the UK. It is special as it is not associated with one geographical area. In the past it was commonly used by newscasters on the BBC. It is also undergoing a process of change and is being affected by 'estuary English'. A key feature of this is the increased use of glottalisation.

Standard American English (SAE) is a term used to describe the dominant variety in the United States, although this is yet to be clearly defined. There are numerous differences in standard English across the States, such as differences in the use of the post-vocalic 'r'.

### Social Dialects

Social dialects need to consider:

>...  occupation, place of residence, education, income, ‘new’ versus ‘old’ money, racial
or ethnic category, cultural background, caste, religion, and so on.
#### Kizdeutch 'neighbourhood German'

The issue of Kiezdeutsch is an emotive one in Germany. Some people view it as a bastardisation of correct German as a result of foreigner not being able to speak the language properly. Others (Wiese) see it as an essential German development that was in part caused by contact with other languages.

Many of its key features are in dispute. Such as whether it is predominantly spoken by younger people or whether it meets the threshold to be considered a dialect.
#### Ethnic dialects

Ethnic dialects are a social construction rather than a biological one.

The case of Pennsylvania German English is an interesting case study. It shows that the group of Dutch German speakers that maintained closer contact with their native language incorporated fewer Dutchisms into their use of English. This can be explained as native speakers of English wishing to maintain their Dutch German identity by other means and thus changing their use of English accordingly.

The term ethnolect can be useful when talking about the self-perception of a speaker of a language variety but should be viewed with suspicion by sociolinguists as a means of describing reality.


#### African American Vernacular English
##### Features of AAVE

Numerous features of AAVE have been identified - not least by Labov. See full article for details.
##### Development of AAVE

There is dispute about the origins of AAVE. Some argue that it has not features that cannot be found in other European based dialects of English. Whereas others view it as a form of creole with roots in Africa. 

Divergence theory is another area of controversy. Are black and white speech diverging, converging or both. And in the are diverging - who is diverging from whom.

Study is ongoing regarding Latino English. This shares some characteristics with AAVE. There are relatively few grammar / vocabulary features that can be seen as unique. Much of what identifies a speaker of Latino English are differences in pronunciation from the standard model, both are the level of the phoneme and supra-segmental features (intonation).
### Styles, Registers and Genres

The terms have been variously described but in general:

> The term style is most often used to discuss differences in formality; register generally denotes specific ways of speaking associated with particular professions or social groups; and genre is understood as a set of co-occurring language features associated with particular frames. 
#### Style

Style is related to formality and the language used in different settings. The use of style is employed across languages is a relatively easy to predict phenomena.
#### Register

>  Use of such registers thus either conforms to the norms for a particular, socially situated way of using language, or is a way of invoking the context usually associated with that register.

For example legalese and personal ads language are examples of register.
#### Genre

Genre is similar to register in that it is used in a particular setting but:

> register focuses more on the social situation, and genre more on the text type

In other words, the language is more easily identifiable through the use of specific language features.
