### Introduction

Regional dialectology is:

>  the systematic study of how a language varies from one area to another

Key points regarding a dialect:

 - the distinction between language and dialect is a political rather than a linguistic
- linguists prefer to use the term ‘variety’.
- dialects of a language are generally considered to be equal
- some linguists believe that the standard variety may have the advantage over others, e.g. in technical vocabulary
- the development of the standard form of a language is a sociohistorical product
- the standard variety is seldom labelled a dialect by non-linguists.
- accent may be considered separately from dialect
- it is possible to speak the standard form of a language while using a regional accent

### Monolingual Dialectology in Europe

Initial studies in Europe focused on ways of creating universal historical laws related to differences between language varieties. This had limited success and a geographic approach became more popular. This focused on drawing boundaries between areas where varieties began and ended.

#### Some pioneers of dialectology

Large scale surveys of language use took place Germany and France in the late nineteenth century.
#### Drawing and interpreting dialect maps

> A key feature of dialectology is the isogloss: a line drawn on a map separating areas according to particular linguistic features.

##### Major dialect areas

Sometimes several language features diverge on either side of a particular line. This is considered a major dialect boundary.
##### Centres of prestige

The traditional view was that language change spread in a wave from one area to another. More recently Trudgill has posited the idea that change can jump from one urban area to another and then spread outward from these centres.
##### Relic areas

Geographically distant areas can display similar usages. This is because they both demonstrate the older pattern of usage that has not been affected by language change emanating from a nearby urban area.
##### Transitional areas

Transitional areas are those that show signs of influence from one or more neighbouring regions.

#### Criticisms of traditional dialectology

There are a number of criticisms of dialectology. Namely that:

- it predominantly focuses on non-mobile, older, rurual, males and so may not give a true reflection of language use in an area.
- it treat languages items in isolation without considering a wider context.
- its research methods may be inadequate for capturing a true picture of language use in an area.
