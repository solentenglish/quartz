### Key Concepts

> ... language, society, and sociolinguistics research findings must all be viewed in their social contexts, interpreted and redefined.

Sociolinguistics is not unique and does not operate outside of a social context.

### Knowledge of Language

The difference between prescriptive and descriptive grammars, e.g. the distinction between 'few' and 'less'. Communication is possible because of shared knowledge between speakers.

### Competence and Performance

Chomskyian view:

> Linguistic theory is concerned primarily with an ideal speaker-listener, in a completely homogeneous speech-community, who knows its language perfectly and is unaffected by such grammatically irrelevant conditions as memory limitations, distractions, shifts of attention and interest, and errors (random or characteristic) in applying his knowledge of the language in actual performance. ... [4] To study actual linguistic performance, we must consider the interaction of a variety of factors, of which the underlying competence of the speaker-hearer is only one. ...

Sociolinguists are concerned with language as it is used in real life situations not an idealised version of the language.

According to Hymes: being competent in a language means knowing not only how to form correct sentences but also when, where, and with whom to use them. This is known as communicative competence.
### Variation

There is variety across regions and among people. People vary their speech for a variety of reasons.

> ... variation is an inherent characteristic of all languages at all times, and the patterns exhibited in this variation carry social meanings.

There are limits to the amount of change that is possible.

### Speakers and their Groups

Identity is defined as membership of one or more social group. Identity can be fluid and complex. What is means to be a member of a group can also change over time.

Groups have a number of dimension to consider, e.g. long-lasting or temporary, formal or informal, close-knit or casual, large or small. We may see some aspects of our identity as more salient than others.

Powers has an important role when considering identity. In terms of language, speaking the standard language in a community may bring advantages.

### Idiolects

An idiolect is an individuals unique way of speaking. This can be described as a narrative by an individual taking into account things such as their education and the places they have lived.

### Language and Culture

#### Directions of Influence

Language and culture can be related in a number of ways. Either your social context determines that way that you speak or that your language determines social structure or worldview.

Children and adults use different language so it would be safe to assume that it is age that is determining the way people speak and not vice versa.

An example of the opposite type of relationship is sexist language. It is hoped that a society that eschews sexist language will become less sexist as a result.

A third possible relationship a bi-directional one. Language influence culture which in turn leads to changes in language.

A final possibility is that there is not relationship between the two.

#### The Whorfian Hypothesis

The Whorfian hypothesis is that language determines our social reality, e.g. languages with a lot of words for a particular subject the speakers will find it easier to speak about that subject. This might especially be the case with technical language. Another claim in that you will perceive the world differently depending on how your language divides the world in separate groups, e.g colours.

Whorf argued that Hopi people and those of Standard Average European countries viewed time differently due to the grammatical structure of their respective languages.

This has been much criticised but there have been other more interesting studies, i.e. ones about the way people talk about the relative location of objects.
#### Correlations

> Ultimately, the goal of sociolinguistics is to address the social meanings of language use, and correlation with social variables is one way to address this question.

We need to ask questions regarding causality and correlation of the findings we uncover. For example, if women tend to use more standard forms of language, this may not be something innate to women but may have another cause.

### The Boundaries of Sociolinguistics

A consideration of the difference between sociology of language (work done by sociologists in order to better understand society) and sociolinguistics (work done by linguists to better understand language). There is also the work done by critical sociologists which has a political foundation in its exploration of language and power differentials.

### Methodological Concerns

There are a number of different types of study that attempt to shed light on the relationship between language and culture.
#### Data

Data can be gathered formally through methods such as surveys or in the wild by various observational methods. There exists the observer's paradox in that we are trying to ascertain the language that people use when they are not being observed.

#### Research Design

Qualitative and quantitative research each brings its own set of challenges when designing studies.
