Succinct History and Overview of U.S. Sociolinguistics

## Definition of Sociolinguistics

The difference between sociology of language sociolinguistics:

> The former addresses language's affect on society while the latter is concerned with society's effect on language.

Sociolinguistics can operate on both the macro and the micro level, i.e. at the level of pronunciation variation and also of government policy towards language death.

## Early history of Sociolinguistics

One of the early theorists was Dell Hymes who first employed the term 'communicative competence'. This was a reaction to Chomsky's narrow definition of language competence.

There are also listed various other academics, journals and universities who played an important role in the United States.

## Language Variation and Dialectology

Gives a definition of language:

> Language is an arbitrary system of vocal or visual symbols used by members of a speech community to communicate.

Compare this with Chomsky's definition:

> A language is  "a set (finite or infinite) of sentences, each finite in length and constructed out of finite set of elements."

Standard language is an important concept - whether it exists in the minds users, dictionaries or formal government bodies.

A dialect is a mutually intelligible form of the language that may differ in terms of pronunciation, syntax, lexis etc.

Dialects are commonly thought of in terms of class and region but there have been a number of additional -lects that have been identified e.g.

acrolect - highest version
basilect - lowest version
ethnolect - spoken by an ethnic group
rurilect - spoken in the countryside
urbilect - spoken in the city
genderlect - spoken by a particular gender

## Pidgins and Creoles

A pidgin is a simplified combination of two languages for means of communication. An invader's language is often referred to as a superstrate and the conquered as the substrate.

A creole is when a pidgin is spoken as a native language by a new generation of speakers. There are various explanations as the origins of pidgins.

## Bilingualism and Multilingualism

There are various definitions of this.

> Simply stated bilingualism refers to the appropriate use of two languages according to social context.

There are questions on both the micro and macro level around degrees of bilingualism in the individual an also state support for one or other language in a bilingual state.

> Multilingualism, or plurilingualism, refers to the situation in which an individual employs two or more languages according to the social situation.

## Code Switching

Code switching is the sophisticated use of two languages in the same speech situation.

There a listed various conditions under which code switching may take place.
## Language Attitudes

Comparison of good and bad languages in the context of prescriptive and descriptive grammars. Definitions of 'euphemism' and 'dysphemism'.

## Language Planning

> Language planning means the intentional attempt to have an effect on the acquisition, form and function of a language or a language variety in the context of a speech community.

This could mean things such as language spread, language revival, language maintenance, language reform etc.
## Language Death

This is an increasing concern as many languages around the world are dying out.
## E-Discourse

This is an emotive issue around the use of unconventional orthography online. Some traditionalists view this as a degradation of the language.
