### English for All

> English has also become a lingua franca to the point that any literate educated person is in a very real sense deprived if he does not know English. Poverty, famine, and disease are instantly recognized as the cruellest and least excusable forms of deprivation. Linguistic deprivation is a less easily noticed condition, but one nevertheless of great significance. (Burchfield 1985: 160)

> English has a dominant position in science, technology, medicine, and computers; in research, books, periodicals, and software; in transnational business, trade, shipping, and avia­tion; in diplomacy and international organizations; in mass media entertainment, news agencies, and journalism; in youth culture and sport

A number of factors have contributed to the spread of English including British Colonialism, the success of the US and the large amounts of money spent propagating the language.

Many countries have loanwords from English to the extent that some countries have tried to prevent the process. 

>  ... are Peace Corps Volunteers who teach English merely teachers, or are they agents of linguistic, and cultural imperialism-an imperialism which may conceivably result in linguistic and cultural genocide?'
