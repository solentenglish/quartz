### Competing Pedagogical Paradigms

Posits that Western ELT has the beliefs that:
- learning is principally a cognitive activity
- outside events are distractions from effective learning
- learning styles are universal

Students, on the other hand, experience:
- controversial views re: power and technology
- cognitive dissonance with reading material
- a hidden curriculum of values 

>  Teachers should therefore attempt to critically interrogate the hidden curricula of their courses, relate learning to the larger socio-political realities, and encourage students to make pedagogical choices that offer sounder alternatives to their living conditions.

##### learning as a detached cognitive activity vs. learning as personal
Emotions are suppressed in mainstream pedagogy (MP).

##### learning as transcendental vs. learning as situated
CP posits that the learner is situated in their environment.

##### learning processes as universal vs. learning as cultural
CP holds that truth is socially constructed. Different traditions of knowing should be negotiated.

##### knowledge as value-free vs. knowledge as ideological
Teachers should interrogate the hidden assumptions in material

##### knowledge as preconstructed vs. knowledge as negotiated
Knowledge should be consciously negotiated in the classroom.

##### learning as instrumental vs. learning as political
Teaching is implicated in the exercise of power.

### The Context of Critical Pedagogy

> CP believes that knowledge is intrinsically social and constructed through interaction between community members.

Mainstream pedagogy is born of Enlightenment beliefs which are now being challenged.
### Academic Reception of Critical Pedagogies

> It suited ELT to define language and teaching as a value-free cognitive activity, since in that way its material and ideological interests in spreading English globally could be conveniently ignored.

### Competing models of Critical Pedagogy

MP can influence people to:

>  to undertake alienating mechanical labor while suppressing other expressive and spiritual concerns, accept hierarchical social arrangements, accommodate the demands of authority figures, and prioritize self-interest.

>  educational processes may make the dominant ideologies appear natural and legitimate, thereby making subordinate groups internalize such values
