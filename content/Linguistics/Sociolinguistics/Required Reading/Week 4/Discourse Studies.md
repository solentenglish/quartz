### Introduction

The introduction positions the article in the performative / post-modern school of sociolinguistics in that it considers gendered language as a aspect of identity that can be constructed by individuals through their choice of language.
### The Emergence Principle

Identity is contextual and malleable. 

> The idea of emergence was promoted early on in linguistic anthropology by Dell Hymes,
whose view of artful linguistic performance as dialogic rather than monologic
led him to call for an understanding of ‘structure as sometimes emergent in
action’ 

Different examples are given to support this view. The adoption of differently gendered language by a transgendered Indian person when relating a narrative and the use of AAVE by a Korean Male in order to contrast their identity with that of whiteness.
### The Positionality Principle

> At the most basic level, identity emerges in discourse through the
temporary roles and orientations assumed by participants, such as evaluator,
joke teller, or engaged listener.

The way that positionality is exemplified is through language like 'go', 'be like', and 'says'. Through the use of language such as this, the speaker can demonstrate their identity in relation to the people whose speech they are reporting.
### The Indexicality Principle

The language we use to describe ourselves or others conveys a sense of identity. This can be seen in the early example of the use of the term 'hijra' - which is normally pejorative in Indian society.

A gay person may avoid gendered terms when describing a partner. This would identify them as gay to a person with a sophisticated understanding of gendered language - so in a sense they are indexing themselves.

When using language to assign someone agency we are indexing them as either a victim of an action or an active participant in a scenario.

A more complex understanding of the concept of style helps us understand the indexicality principle. A speaker may appear to be using a consistent style to the untrained listener but someone with knowledge of the ethnographic nuance of a situation may be able to identify ways in which the speaker is indexing themselves in relation to a particular group.

### The Relationality Principle

Identity is constructed through comparison with others, e.g by making an equivalence between Sadaam Hussain and the 9/11 bombers.

### The Partialness Principle
Any portrayal of identity is always incomplete and dependent on perspective; identities are complex and multi-layered. They are in part consciously constructed.

> Thus both structure and agency are intertwined as components of micro as well
as macro articulations of identity.

---
### Conclusion

> The linguistic resources that indexically produce identity at all these levels are therefore necessarily broad and flexible, including labels, implicatures, stances, styles, and entire languages and varieties.
