### Introductions

- Needs Analysis (NA) is essential in designing effective English for Specific Purposes (ESP) courses.
    
- While NA has a long-standing role in ESP, its **methodology** has often been overlooked or inconsistently applied.
    
- This study examines NA practices from 1984–2014 to highlight **strengths, weaknesses**, and propose **methodological improvements**.
    
- It also presents a **large-scale NA case study** from a scientific research institution in the U.S.
    

---

### Background Information

- The **task-based approach** is recommended for NA because:
    
    - Tasks reflect **real-world activities** that learners must perform.
        
    - They are **meaningful and motivating** for learners.
        
    - They fit well with how adults acquire language (through meaningful use, not isolated structures).
        
- A rigorous task-based NA should:
    
    - Use **multiple sources** (learners, domain experts, ESP teachers).
        
    - Use **multiple methods** (interviews, questionnaires, observation).
        
    - **Triangulate** data (cross-check info from different sources/methods).
        
    - Begin with **open-ended tools** (e.g. interviews), then move to **closed tools** (e.g. structured surveys).
        
- Review of 30 years of NA studies shows:
    
    - Improvement in methods over time.
        
    - Continuing issues: lack of sampling clarity, weak triangulation, limited piloting of tools.

### Current Study

- Conducted at a **U.S. scientific research institution** for international post-docs and researchers.
    
- Aim: Identify their English language needs to design an ESP course.
    

**Steps:**

1. **Step 0**: Institution requested help due to communication issues.
    
2. **Step 1**: Conducted open-ended, semi-structured interviews with:
    
    - In-service learners (trainees)
        
    - Domain experts (supervisors)
        
    - Focused on tasks, difficulties, cultural issues, goals.
        
3. **Step 2**: Designed three tailored questionnaires:
    
    - For non-native speakers, native speakers, and supervisors.
        
    - Closed-ended format for easier analysis.
        
    - Piloted and revised based on feedback.
        
4. **Step 3**: Follow-up included:
    
    - Non-participant observation
        
    - Discourse analysis (e.g. lab meeting transcripts)
        
5. **Step 4**: Triangulated data to identify most frequent, important, and difficult tasks.
    

- The process was iterative, rigorous, and involved **insider and outsider perspectives**.
    
- Highlighted differences in perception between learners and supervisors.
    

---

### Limitations

- **Criticality** of tasks (how important they are) was not measured separately from frequency/difficulty.
    
- **Few open-ended questions** in the final questionnaire (to allow for efficient large-scale analysis).
    
- Participants were **volunteers** (convenience sample), not a randomised sample—may limit generalisability.
    
- **Response rate** for the questionnaire was around 23%, which is slightly below average for email-based surveys.
    

---

### Conclusion and Methodological Checklist

- While NA is widely used, many studies **lack methodological rigour**.
    
- Consistency and careful design are essential for valid and useful results.
    
- A **well-designed NA** supports effective course development, just as a good diagnosis supports effective treatment.
    

#### ✅ Adaptable Methodological Checklist

1. **Sources**
    
    - Combine **insiders** (domain experts, learners) and **outsiders** (linguists, ESP teachers).
        
    - Include both **native and non-native speakers** where relevant.
        
2. **Methods**
    
    - Use both **qualitative and quantitative** methods.
        
    - Follow the **"open before closed"** rule (start inductive, move to deductive).
        
    - **Pilot test** all materials and revise as needed.
        
3. **Triangulation**
    
    - Cross-check data using **multiple sources** and **multiple methods**.
        
    - Consider **cultural factors** as possible sources of miscommunication.
        

> A thorough NA leads to more relevant, effective, and targeted ESP instruction.
