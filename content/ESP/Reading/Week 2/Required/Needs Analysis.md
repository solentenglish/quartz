### Relevance of NA
Needs Analysis (or Needs Assessment) is the first stage in designing an ESP curriculum. The information collected in a NA should guide the development of materials on the course.

### NA and Language Skills
NA can uncover the variety of skills needed in a professions, for example pilots need listening, readings and writing skills in addition to speaking skills.

### Sources and Methods in Needs Analysis

### Organization of Needs Analysis Data

Organising the data collected in a NA can be challenging but it is a key stage in the production of a syllabus.

### Specialized Vocabulary in ESP

Specialised vocabulary in a particular field is known as terminology or terminological units. Learners' need in this regard will be dependent not only on their profession but also on their particular role and experience within this field.

### ESP Learners’ Vocabulary Needs

The variability in learners' needs highlights the importance of need analysis. When dealing with speakers of romance languages, you can often rely upon a number of cognates to help students.

### Dealing With Specialized Vocabulary in ESP Courses

Contextualising new vocabulary with pictures is often a key technique within ESP courses.

### Deciphering Long Terminological Units

Long terminological units can often be a sticking point in ESP courses. These are often nouns with a large degree of pre-modification. There are various techniques that can help with this such as breaking down a large noun phrase into its component pieces. Teaching differences between how such units are formed in a student's L1 is another technique.

### Autonomous Vocabulary Learning Strategies

As teachers are not always experts in a given field, students should be supported in developing their own understanding of terminology.

There are a number of technological resources available. Including finding definitions online, using translation services or searching for images.

Some care needs to be taken, however, as word or phrases may have a generalised meaning that is not the same within a technical context.
