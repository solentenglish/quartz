# Chapter 2: Needs Analysis and ESP Course Design

## Defining Needs Analysis

Need analysis can be defined as either collecting all the data required with which to justify a particular curriculum or as the process of identifying the gap between a learner's current language ability and that of their target language use.

Initial research was focused on language used in the target situation. This broadened over time to include considerations of discourse and genre. The focus on the current state of the learner became a consideration at a later date.

It is worth recognising that a learner's needs and their perceived needs may not always be aligned.

## Stakeholders’ Perspectives

There may be a large number of stakeholders in any given project. These need to be taken into account but it is worth remembering that stakeholders may be operating with incomplete information. They may not now in which situation participants on a course will be using English. Similarly, participants may not now about gaps in their ability, particularly if they are new to a role.

These problems can be overcome by consulting as broad a variety of sources when conducting a needs analysis.
## Methodologies Used in Collecting Data About Needs

The following methodologies are listed:

- Questionnaires and surveys
- Text analysis
- Language assessment
- Previous research filed
- Discussions with stakeholders
- Interviews (unstructured, semi-structured and structured)
- Target-situation observations (participant, non-participant)
- Learning-situation observations
- Learner diaries, logs and journals
- Language audits
- Expert and non-expert intuitions
- Role-plays and simulations
- Corpus analysis
- Ethnography

Rather than simply using surveys, a variety of approaches should be triangulated.

### Settings of needs analysis

Within EAP, there is likely to be less flexibility as academic norms are relatively set. The focus here is likely to be on the students current ability.

Within EOP, there is more scope for course design. Research may involve more ethnographic techniques. 
### The learner and needs analysis

Placing learners at the centre of the needs of analysis can create greater buy-in from learners.

### Moving from needs analysis to course design

It's necessary to move from the analysis on to course design. This is sometimes represented as a linear process but students' needs change over time.

# Chapter 4: Language and Skills in ESP

ESP courses are less likely to be organised according to a grammar syllabus than General English courses. The focus is more likely to be on skills.

It is useful to think of language in terms of competencies:

- Grammatical competence
- Sociolinguistic knowledge
- Discoursal competence
- Strategic competence

### Specificity of content and language

ESP courses can be more or less specific, e.g. teaching general academic English or teaching general business English.

### ESP and grammar

On reason that grammar is often neglected in ESP is the heterogeneity of the learners. Students of different levels may be present in the same class. For more advanced learners, it may be assumed that learners already know the grammar. This is why it's often left to teachers to supplement with grammar on a course.

### ESP and functions and notions in language

The CEF has a comprehensive list of functions applicable to language learnings.

### Skills

Selecting which skills to focus on is key to designing and ESL course.

#### Writing

Writing is especially important in EAP and there is extensive literature on the subject.
#### Reading

Selecting appropriate reading material, usually authentic texts, is also important.
#### Speaking

A great deal of business is done in English. Teaching speaking in ESP does not always mean adhering to native speaker norms.
#### Listening

Working with various sub-skills for listening is important in ESP.

1 Listen for details
2 Listen for main ideas
3 Listen for global understanding
4 Listen and infer
5 Listen and predict
6 Listen selectively

# Chapter 4: Vocabulary and ESP

Vocabulary in ESP is often divided into technical, semi-technical, and general. It is important to bear in mind multi word units. The use of corpora has made these easier to identify.

### Vocabulary levels

It is important to consider vocabulary level. Much work has been done on this in regard to EAP.

### Teacher vocabulary knowledge

This can be challenging as course designers are often not specialists in the area. There are various ways to overcome this. Vocabulary lists are often available but increasingly corpora are being used to identify key terms.

### Teaching vocabulary

You need to consider whether the vocabulary you are teaching is for productive or receptive use.

### Vocabulary and learners

Teachers may present a range of learning strategies which can help students learn vocabulary.
