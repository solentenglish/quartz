### Introduction

Dates the history of ESP to 1977 and the work of Strevens

### A Definition and Discussion of Principal Components

Strevens states the following:

> A definition of ESP needs to distinguish between four absolute and two variable characteristics:
> 
> **1) Absolute characteristics:**  
> ESP consists of English language teaching which is:  
> - designed to meet specified needs of the learner  
> - related in content (i.e., in its themes and topics) to particular disciplines, occupations and activities  
> - centered on the language appropriate to those activities in syntax, lexis, discourse, semantics, etc., and analysis of this discourse  
> - in contrast with "General English"
> 
> **2) Variable characteristics:**  
> ESP may be, but is not necessarily:  
> - restricted as to the language skills to be learned (e.g., reading only)  
> - not taught according to any pre-ordained methodology
> 
> **Claims:** The claims for ESP are:  
> - being focussed on the learner's need, wastes no time  
> - is relevant to the learner  
> - is successful in imparting learning  
> - is more cost-effective than "General English"

#### Needs analysis

The development of needs analysis is ongoing and there is the recognition that capturing this data is a complex process.

#### Discourse analysis

- **Discourse analysis** in ESP examines authentic written or spoken language to inform curriculum design.
    
- The focus is often on **authentic/genuine language** used in specific professional or academic contexts.
    
- **Lexicostatistics (word/item counts)** are widely used, with increasing sophistication:
    
    - Includes **tense/aspect/mood functions**, **communicative notions**, and **concordancing**.
        
- **University of Washington School** (Trimble, Selinker, Tarone, etc.) made key contributions:
    
    - Studied the **scientific passive** in astrophysics journals.
        
    - Validated findings with field experts—an ongoing practice in ESP.
        
- **Communicative syllabus design** influenced the communicative notions approach:
    
    - Kennedy (1987) used multiple methods to identify ~200 devices for temporal frequency in texts.
        
- **Concordancing tools** help students explore how language is used in context:
    
    - Used in Oman (Stevens, 1991) and Zimbabwe (Mparutsa et al., 1991).
        
- **Rhetorical Process Charts** (Trimble, 1985) aid in identifying discourse levels in texts.
    
- **Swales' genre analysis** defines structured steps in research paper introductions:
    
    - Establish field → Describe previous research → Indicate gap → Introduce current research.
        
- Other researchers focused on specific disciplines:
    
    - **Dubois** (biomedicine), **Bazerman** (physics), **Dudley-Evans & Henderson** (economics).
        
- **Learner-centered approaches** emphasise interaction with discourse:
    
    - Students may understand words but miss meaning or purpose (Olsen & Huckin, Daoud).
        
    - St. John (1987) examined challenges faced by Spanish scientists writing in English.
        
- Overall, both **external text features** and **learner interaction** are essential in ESP discourse analysis.

### International Scope

> the language problem in development stems from at least three communication needs which are increasingly being recognized both in developing countries themselves and in other countries aiding in their development: internal communication, transmission of science and technology, and international communication. 

In developing countries English is often a 'neutral' language of communication that plays a key role in education.

A knowledge of English is rapidly becoming essential in the sciences due to the number of papers published in this language.

English is the lingua franca in the aviation and shipping industries.

### Issues and Controversies

Initial discussions were around whether ESP was a profitable approach to take with students. Now that this question is largely moot, discussion has move on to whether we should take a narrow angle approach or focus open a common core.

Studies have shown that, despite some similarities, language is used differently in different disciplines, e.g. between anthropology and sociology.

There is also the question of which skills should be taught. Reading is the skill most in demand and there have been successful programmes that have focused on teaching this one skill.

Writing is also an important academic skill which mean the product / process debate is relevant to the field of ESP.

### Future Developments

ESP is following in the footsteps of business English coursebooks and courses but it may also have a role outside of the classroom.

> ESP may, in fact, begin to expand beyond its classroom role to assume a substantial consultancy role in a wide variety of academic and professional environments
