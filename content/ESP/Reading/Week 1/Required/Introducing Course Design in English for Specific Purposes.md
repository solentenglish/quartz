### Overview of English for Specific Purposes (ESP)
### What is ESP?

> [!info] Definition of ESP p.5
> The absolute characteristics of ESP courses are:
> - Designed to meet the needs of the learner. 
> - Make use of the methodology and activities of the disciplines it serves. 
> - Centred on the language, skills and genres appropriate to these disciplines.
>
> The variable characteristics of ESP courses are:
> - May be related to specific disciplines. 
> - May use different methodologies to English for general purposes (EGP). 
> - Likely to be designed for adult learners. 
> - Generally designed for intermediate or advanced learners.

> [!info] Some typical characteristics of ESP and EGP courses p.6
> | **ESP** | **EGP** |
> |---|---|
> | Adult learners | Any age learners |
> | Learners have a common goal | Learners have a wide range of goals |
> | Learners have high external motivation | Learners have a range of motivation |
> | Are short term | Are long term |
> | Based on needs analysis | May not bear in mind student needs |
> | Have an academic, professional or workplace focus | Have no specific focus |
> | Have specific content | Have general content |
> | Are based on specific target communication | May include limited exposure to target communicative events |
> | Learners usually have a basic command of English | Learners may be at all levels, including absolute beginners |
> | Focus on specific lexis | A full range of lexis is included |
> | May have a limited focus on grammar | Usually incorporates the full grammatical system. This may be the central organisational strand in the syllabus |
> | A limited range of skills taught – for example, writing and speaking | All four skills are taught with equal focus |
> | ESP courses may be taught with methodologies relevant to the content field | Methodology tends to be similar across courses adopting current thinking in English-language teaching in the region |

### Classification of ESP

ESP is generally classified as either EAP (English for Academic Purposes) or EOP (English for Occupational Purposes).

Other subdivision exist in these categories.

EAP can EGAP (English for General Academic Purposes), e.g studying for IELTS / TOEFL or (ESAP) English for Medical Students.

EOP can be further subdivided based upon the work experience of those taking the course or whether it is related to a professional (e.g. law) or vocational (e.g. tourism) subject.

### History of ESP

**1960s** – Grammar and vocabulary of scientific texts.

**1970s** – Rhetorical functions like comparison and cause-effect in scientific texts.

**Late 1970s** – Needs analysis based on communicative situations.

**1980s** – Study skills and a learner-centred approach.

**1990s** – Genre analysis and vocational ESP.

**2000s onwards** – Corpus-based analysis to identify patterns in real texts.

### The Role of English

ESP is also being influenced by the concept of ELF (English as a Lingua Franca). This it the concept that English is no longer being learnt as a means of communicating with native speakers but as a means of communicating between people who do not share another common language.

Initial this was mostly defined in geographical terms (Kachru) but, as English grows ever more dominant, proficiency is arguable the key factor.

### Types of ESP
#### EST (English for Science and Technology)
The general focus is on the technical vocabulary used by scientists in academic texts. One issues is the mismatch between the backgrounds of teachers and students. Most teachers have a background in humanities rather than the sciences.

#### EAP (English for Academic Purposes)
Initially, courses tend to focus on preparing students for exams that are required for study at university, e.g. IELTS / TOEFL.

Beyond that, there is discussion regarding how specific course should be. Are EGAP worthwhile if the courses students are attending focus on different text types or rhetorical devices. Are the skills learnt on EGAP courses transferable to specific disciplines.

#### BE (Business English)
Globally, it is very common for international business to be conducted in English. This is why there is such interest in BELF (Business English as a Lingua Franca).

Cambridge offers separate exams in Business English.

#### EOP (English for Occupational Purposes)
This is likely to be very focused on the language required to complete specific workplace tasks or on key areas such as health and safety.

#### EMP (English for Medical Purposes)
One driver of this is the migration of medical workers. There is a focus on developing an appropriate bedside manner.

This is also true of the nursing profession:

> Differing cultures have different norms concerning death, illness and sexuality. It is important nurses understand the norms of the target community, be these academic norms of supervisors or those of patient

#### ELP (English for Legal Purposes)
Legal language poses problems due to the specificity and complexity of the language involved.

#### ESP (English for Socio Linguistic Purposes)
The goal here is to help with community membership, e.g. to help refugees assimilate when arriving in a new country.
