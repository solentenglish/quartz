### English for General Purposes
English for General Purposes means English without a specified goal. Curriculum exist but they are generally developed without any input from the learners. This makes sense in some cases, e.g. children, but adult might be expected to be consulted. They tend to focus on listening and speaking skills..

### English for Specific Purposes
English for Specific Purposes curriculum can not be developed in a vacuum and will differ widely depending on who is being taught.

### Types of ESP
ESP is sometimes divided into EAP (English for Academic Purposes) and EOP (English for Occupational Purposes). It is worth remembering that no two EOP courses will look the same. They often differ in terms of the experience of who is being taught.

### Teachers’ and Students’ Roles in ESP
Teachers do not need to be experts in the field in order to teach on an ESP course.

### Materials in EGP and ESP Courses

> [!info] Summary of Differences Between an EGP Textbook and an ESP Textbook
> **EGP** vs **ESP**
>
> **Topics**  
> • EGP: General life (e.g., identity)  
> • ESP: Specific to the occupation (e.g., financial industry)  
>
> **Skills**  
> • EGP: Focus on all four skills  
> • ESP: Selective emphasis (e.g., writing is not dealt with in the first two units)  
>
> **Texts**  
> • EGP: Interactive, social, and informal (e.g., discussions, telephone conversations, introductions, arguments)  
> • ESP: Informative (e.g., financial industry development), transactional (e.g., arrangements), and formal (e.g., professional and customer)  
>
> **Language content**  
> • EGP: General knowledge of English (e.g., adverbials, order of adjectives)  
> • ESP: Key aspects needed for the target professionals (e.g., words related to banking products/services)  
>
> **Activities**  
> • EGP: Games, anecdotes, songs  
> • ESP: Role-plays
